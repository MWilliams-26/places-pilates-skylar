### Competitive context (Proposed reading)

NYC studios that market to performers tend to be classical and serious in tone, for example [The Pilates Room NYC](https://www.thepilatesroomnyc.com/feeds/service/performance-sport-pilates) (Chelsea, "no music or social recording"), [Will Taylor Studio](https://willtaylorstudio.com/instructors) (Flatiron, founded by a Broadway veteran), and [Hell's Kitchen Pilates](https://www.hellskitchenpilates.com/) (dancer-instructors). The non-profit [Pilates for Performers](https://www.pilatesforperformers.org/) names cost as the main barrier for artists. Places can stand apart by being **affordable, social, and joyful** while still taking the method seriously, which is exactly what Skylar's own messaging already says.

## 3. Creative direction: how the collage translates to the web

**Concept (Proposed):** "An independent arts magazine that happens to be a Pilates studio." Find Your Place / Take Your Place appear as creative lines, not approved slogans.

- **Kept from the brand guide:** the supplied wordmark and P/star monogram (traced to SVG for crisp rendering; originals in `brand-assets/`), and the five brand colors. Burgundy #571310 is the ink and anchor, brick #A72913 is for actions, blush #FCD3C5 and salmon #FAA994 are paper, and gold #E8AF59 is a sparing highlight (tape, stars, focus rings on dark).
- **Type (from the brand guide):** headlines use the primary font **Choret Fudyng** (Alit Design; the guide spells it "Chores Fudyng"). The licensed web font (Regular style) is installed. **Code** (Fontfabric, free commercial license) handles labels, navigation, buttons, and thin all-caps statements, as in "PILATES" in the wordmark. Code is caps-only, so paragraphs use Instrument Sans for readability. Caveat handles the sparing handwritten notes.
- **Color:** only the five supplied colors plus white. Burgundy is the ink, brick is for actions, blush and salmon are paper, and gold is for highlights.
- **Collage kit, used with restraint:** taped prints, arch cutouts, torn-paper edges, index cards, a ticket stub, hand-drawn arrows and a circled word. At most one collage moment per section. The hero, founder, classes, and community sections carry the most. Pricing, booking, and FAQ stay clean.
- **Mood-board pieces as collage objects:** after review, the hero moved closer to the mood board. It sits on a peach board with the P monogram, a typed script title page beside the founder photo ("script instead of equipment"), a photographic Broadway theatre marquee (concept image) with the real wordmark set on its blank sign face, a taped black-and-white rehearsal print, and the red-telephone dancer with "this is your call to places". The chorus line runs across the community band, and an "Extra! Extra!" front page sits in the theatre seats ("Chicago style"). The script, the newspaper, and the text on the marquee are HTML, so they stay real and accessible. Full-screen curtains and countdown structures stay out.
- **The P monogram** anchors the hero and appears as a sticker on every page header, the founder photo, the script page, the newspaper masthead, and the footer. On dark backgrounds it gets a cream die-cut edge rather than being recolored.
- **Mobile:** collages collapse to one photo plus the monogram sticker. Strips become swipeable rows. The Book button stays in the header and at the end of every page.
- **Imagery:** the frames marked "Photo to come" say exactly what to shoot. Generated concept images (theatre seats, plus three people pieces: the chorus line, the phone dancer, and the B&W rehearsal) stand in for the mood board's photography. Each people image carries a visible "Concept image" tag, and all are listed in `ASSETS.md`. They are never presented as Places photography.

## 4. Website structure (as built in the mockup)

Home · About Skylar · Classes (group, private, virtual) · Schedule & booking (live, on Skylar's Acuity) · Pricing (provisional) · Community · Contact & FAQ · **Studio** (`/studio`, a preview not linked in navigation; switched on with `features.studioInNav` in `src/content.ts`).

The studio page holds slots for location and directions, studio photography, schedule, instructors, policies, and events, plus a *proposed* launch interest list (prototype, stores nothing). No address, opening date, presale, or membership is shown.

Booking: the Schedule page is live booking in the site's own design, on Skylar's "Places, Pilates" Acuity account (owner 37370862). It reads her real session types, packages, and open times through a small same-origin proxy (`server/acuity.ts`: a Cloudflare Worker in production, Vite middleware in dev), because Acuity's public scheduling endpoints send no CORS headers. Payment happens in Acuity's own checkout for the chosen slot, embedded on the page with the client's details prefilled. Packs open Acuity's checkout for that product. The closing newspaper on every page lists her next three open times, each linking straight into booking. New Acuity session types (for example group reformer) appear automatically; today only the private session is bookable, so group and virtual formats link to Contact. The proxy relies on Acuity's undocumented public scheduler endpoints; Phase 2 should move to Acuity's official API or the chosen platform. `src/booking/provider.ts` can still switch to the prototype, Acuity's plain embed, or a link.

Photography: About, Community, and the home page use real photos and posts from @placespilates (see `docs/ASSETS.md`), credited on the page. Generated images remain only where they set a mood (marquee, dancer, theatre seats, chorus line) and carry a "Concept image" tag.

## 6. Recommended technology strategy (Proposed)

| Scenario | Need | Sensible choice |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Lean (today)** | Reliable booking for privates, virtual sessions, and occasional groups; packs; payments; reminders | **Consolidate onto one system now.** The cheapest move is to keep Acuity (already Places-branded, packages set up), retire Wix Bookings, and fix the published prices. Better: start on the platform she would grow into (B/C) if its solo tier is affordable. |
| **B. Growing** | More group classes, a second instructor, memberships, intro offers, waitlists | **Arketa** (published $49 solo tier that grows into studio plans) or **Momence** (strong waitlists and reformer booking, quote-based). |
| **C. Physical studio** | Equipment-based capacity, spot booking, instructor scheduling, payroll reporting, marketing automation | Arketa Studio or Momence again, avoiding a migration; **Mariana Tek** only if she wants a premium custom booking front end and has the budget. |

**Recommendation:** shortlist **Arketa and Momence** as "one platform from A to C". Book both demos with Skylar, using the checklist in section 5. Keep Acuity running until one is live. Choose Mindbody only if marketplace discovery matters more to her than cost.

**Website integration:** whichever platform wins, the site keeps its own design and embeds the platform's schedule widget or links to its hosted checkout. No custom scheduling engine, payments, or CRM.
